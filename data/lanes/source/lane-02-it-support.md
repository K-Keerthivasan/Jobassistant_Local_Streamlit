# Lane 2 | IT support, help desk, technical support

NOC 22221 and 22220, TEER 2. Priority lane. Read `00-index.md` for the Status conventions and tier rules.

**PR note.** This is his broadest-pathway lane. 22221 is on the Brandon RCIP list and was on Manitoba's MPNP list as of v3.1 (re-verify). 22220 is on the North Bay and Sault Ste. Marie RCIP lists. AIP covers both from a designated Atlantic employer, and Ontario's Workforce Priority stream takes either with a qualifying offer. More live routes than any other lane he has.

## Not required

Security operations, firewall administration, and anything requiring a clearance. A posting hard-requiring Reliability Status or a clearance is a credential-gate drop regardless of skill.

## Honest positioning

This is his strongest lane on paper and it should be treated that way. The 95 percent first-contact resolution figure is concrete, unusual and quotable, and it leads every application in this lane. The Bill Gosling year adds the thing most junior support candidates cannot show: a track record of holding a conversation with an unhappy person and still landing the outcome.

Most postings in this lane grade Tier A. The two gaps that recur are **PowerShell** and **Intune or SCCM**, and closing either would remove the most common named requirement he misses. Where a posting leans network rather than end-user, it is likely 22220 and likely a stretch, so name the networking gap rather than glossing it.

## Interview watch

Expect a scenario: a user cannot log in, walk me through it. Answer it as a structured diagnostic, not a guess list, and say out loud when you would escalate and why. Expect a question about handling a queue when everything is urgent. The first-contact resolution number is the natural place to anchor that answer.
