# Lane 9 | Video and multimedia production

NOC 52119 or 52110, TEER 2. Track 2. Read `00-index.md` for the Status conventions and tier rules.

**PR note.** Both codes are TEER 2 and clear the floor. **No RCIP list covers either, so never tag a lane 9 role RCIP.** AIP covers them from a designated Atlantic employer. Ontario's Workforce Priority stream takes them with a qualifying offer.

## Not required

Feature-film credits, union membership, a film degree. A posting requiring any of them is a credential-gate drop.

## Honest positioning

Strong lane on portfolio, weaker on credentials, which is the usual shape for production work and it is fine because this lane hires on reel. **The reel is the application.** Lead with Reels for ad-creative and social roles, and with the scripted channel work for anything narrative or content-led. The Unreal Engine work is genuinely unusual for a junior video candidate and worth surfacing wherever a posting touches real-time, virtual production or game content.

The gap worth naming is crewed production: he has been a one-person operation, so he has not worked as part of a set or a post team with a producer, a schedule and someone else's brief. Most in-scope postings grade Tier A.

**One standing caution carried from lane 7.** Do not let this lane's strength pull marketing applications toward camera work. A video-first framing on a digital marketing posting costs him the 11202 mapping and reads as the wrong hire.

## Interview watch

Expect a reel review and expect to be asked what you would change about a piece in it. Pick the honest answer rather than defending the work. Expect a turnaround question: how fast can you cut a Reel from raw footage. Have a real number.
