## The standing rule for this lane

**Do not stretch into this lane to fill a run.** A role enters only where the posting is genuinely design-led and he can evidence it. This lane exists to catch the occasional real match, not to pad a thin batch, and it is the first thing to skip when a run is short.

## Not required

A design degree, and formal UX credentials. A posting hard-requiring either is a credential-gate drop.

## Honest positioning

This is his weakest lane and the matrix says so honestly. He produces design output as part of delivering web and content work, which is real, but he is a developer and marketer who designs rather than a designer. The missing piece is not taste or tooling, it is a **presented design portfolio**: design roles hire on a body of work shown as design decisions, and his work is currently shown as websites and campaigns.

On current statuses most design postings grade Tier C and get dropped, which is correct. If he wants this lane to produce, the move is a portfolio, not more skills. Until then, keep it opportunistic and let lanes 7, 8 and 9 carry Track 2.

## Interview watch

Expect a portfolio walkthrough and expect to be asked why, not what. Design interviews test the reasoning behind a choice. If the honest answer is that the client asked for it, say so rather than inventing a rationale, and pivot to a decision that was actually yours.
