# Lane 12 | VFX artist, junior

New in v3.5. NOC 52111, 52119 or 52120, all TEER 2. Read `00-index.md` for the Status conventions and tier rules.

**PR note.** All three candidate codes are TEER 2 and clear the floor, so this lane is PR-worthy but sits a tier below lane 11. **No RCIP community list covers any of them, so never tag a lane 12 role RCIP.** AIP covers the lane from a designated Atlantic employer with no occupation list, and Ontario's Workforce Priority stream takes any NOC with a qualifying offer, which matters because the Ontario VFX market is where the jobs are.

## NOC map

| NOC | Title | TEER | When it applies |
|---|---|---|---|
| **52111** | Graphic arts technicians | 2 | What Job Bank maps "VFX Artist" to. The default for a compositing, roto, cleanup or general VFX seat. |
| 52119 | Other technical and coordinating occupations in motion pictures, broadcasting and performing arts | 2 | Lists **"special effects technician"** as an illustrative example. Fits on-set and practical-adjacent VFX work. |
| 52120 | Graphic designers and illustrators | 2 | Art-led and motion-design-led seats. |

Decide from the duties as always. A compositor is 52111. A special effects technician working on set is 52119. A motion graphics designer is 52120.

## Credentials

- **Visual Effects and Editing for Contemporary Media, Fanshawe College, London ON (2024 to 2025).** A named VFX credential, recently completed. This is a real differentiator: most junior VFX applicants are self-taught and this lane still respects programme training.
- **Portfolio site: film.kkvasan.ca**, already built and live, with categories and video embeds.

## Not required

Feature credits, union membership, or a degree beyond the Fanshawe programme. A posting requiring shipped feature credits has failed the seniority gate.

## Sourcing vocabulary

- **Core:** junior VFX artist, junior compositor, compositor, roto artist, paint artist, cleanup artist, VFX assistant, junior motion graphics artist, match move artist, VFX generalist.
- **Adjacent and worth opening:** virtual production artist, real-time artist, Unreal artist, technical artist, motion designer, post-production artist. **Virtual production and real-time are where his Unreal work makes him unusually competitive** and the postings are less contested than roto seats.
- **Geography reality:** Canadian VFX concentrates in **Vancouver and Toronto**, with Montreal third and usually French-gated. Toronto is the realistic target from London for hybrid or onsite, Vancouver for remote or relocation. **Remote junior VFX is rare** because studios run security-controlled pipelines, so expect onsite or hybrid requirements and treat a fully-remote junior comp posting with some suspicion.
- Studio names worth watching directly in an employer-first pass: the major Vancouver and Toronto facilities post on their own careers pages more reliably than they appear on aggregators.

## Honest positioning

Better supported than it looks. He has a **recent named VFX credential**, a **live portfolio site**, **Nuke**, and specific named project work across the exact tasks junior VFX roles hire for: roto, cleanup, object removal, motion tracking, object replacement, lock-off compositing. That is a real junior VFX profile, not an aspiration.

Two things make him unusual rather than typical. **Unreal Engine plus VFX** points straight at virtual production, which is the growth end of the industry. **Python plus VFX** points at pipeline and technical-artist work, which studios struggle to hire and which pays better than roto. Both are worth leading with over the standard "I can composite" framing, because the standard framing puts him in a queue behind everyone else with the same diploma.

The honest gaps are **Maya**, **shot tracking software** and **studio pipeline experience**, all of which are learned on the job and none of which should stop an application. Most in-scope postings grade Tier A or Tier B.

**The reel is the application.** In this lane more than any other, the portfolio decides the outcome and the resume barely matters. Breakdowns matter more than finished shots: show the plate, the roto, the elements and the comp, because that is what a VFX supervisor is actually reading for.

## Interview watch

Expect a reel and breakdown review, and expect to be asked exactly what you did on each shot, because reels routinely overstate. Answer precisely and claim only your own work. Expect a question about handling notes and versions, which is really a question about whether you will be difficult in a review. Expect to be asked about turnaround and volume, since junior comp work is measured in shots per week. For virtual production and technical-artist seats, lead with Unreal and Python and let the compositing be the supporting evidence.
